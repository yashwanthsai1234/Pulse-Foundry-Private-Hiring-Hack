# B3 - real-world files (breaker W3-B3)

Scope: files not produced by our generator. 7 real/derived CSVs, 10 schedule PDFs made by Chrome, PyMuPDF, LibreOffice and a scan simulation, 2 real PDFs from the web. Everything ran through the real `Pipeline` with `agents = "off"`. For PDFs the e1 CSVs were ingested in the same run (they supply the 2026-09-14 week anchor; see B3-08). Ground truth for generated PDFs: `backend/tests/fixtures/realworld/pdf/truth.json` (14 staff, 2 facilities, 65 non-OFF shifts).

Files: fixtures `backend/tests/fixtures/realworld/` (sources + licences in `SOURCES.md`); generator `backend/tests/breakers/rw_make_pdfs.py`; harness `rw_harness.py`; tests `backend/tests/breakers/test_realworld_{csv,pdf,names}.py`. Run: `cd backend && .venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/breakers/test_realworld_*.py`. Result at time of writing: 19 failed (evidence), 15 pass (the passing ones are the positive controls listed below). Nothing in `sot/` or `packs/` was touched.

### Measurement (question 3)

- Of the 10 generated schedule PDFs: 5 fully correct with no help (50%), 1 partly wrong (hyphen), 1 silently 14% of the data, 1 silently nothing, 2 scans that need the agent (so 0% unaided, by design).
- Of the 8 text-layer ones: 5/8 = 62.5% correct. All 5 successes are ruled grids or coloured/filled cell grids; method: pdf.lines 3, pdf.mupdf 2. `pdf.words` and `pdf.text` never won on any of these files.
- 2 of the 3 failures among the text PDFs are silent (no issue, no task), 1 of those with score 1.00.
- Of all schedule-shaped inputs that are not correct, only the scans are visible to the user. Two real web PDFs: 1 mishandled with 0.80 confidence, 1 handled correctly.
- The score is not a safe signal: `score_grid = header_score x cell_validity x shape` checks only that the cells present look like shift tokens. It never compares the number of rows with what is on the page, and the cascade stops at the first method that reaches 0.80.

## Research notes

- pdfplumber: `lines` strategy uses ruling lines and rectangle edges, `text` strategy infers from word alignment; borderless/shaded tables are the documented weak case: https://github.com/jsvine/pdfplumber#table-extraction-settings and https://github.com/jsvine/pdfplumber/issues/242
- PyMuPDF `find_tables` pitfalls (missing borders, multi-line cells read as extra rows): https://artifex.com/blog/solving-common-issues-with-table-detection-and-extraction and https://medium.com/@pymupdf/solving-common-issues-with-table-detection-and-extraction-4df5de2b8d88
- Camelot Lattice (ruled) vs Stream (whitespace) flavours and per-table accuracy/whitespace reports: https://github.com/camelot-dev/camelot
- General statement that borderless tables rely on whitespace and degrade under real-world variation: https://www.useanvil.com/ask-anvil/pdfs/pdf-table-extraction-jumbled-columns/
- Producer differences seen here: Chrome emits fills as rects and no lines when borders are absent; Excel/LibreOffice emit ruled lines (extract cleanly). These matched the pass/fail split above.

## Recommended order of fixes

1. B3-01 + B3-02 (a completeness term in the score and a task/issue for zero or low tables) - this makes every PDF failure visible, which is the contract of the project ("surface anything wrong").
2. B3-03 (explained-share penalty in confidence).
3. B3-05 (de-hyphenation) and B3-06 (Unicode name validators).
